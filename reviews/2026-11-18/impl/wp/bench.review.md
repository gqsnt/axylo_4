2. **B · The streaming and resize results cannot establish that no frame was late.** **Ran Node on raw history and synthetic timestamps.** P3’s initial chart and settle have a separate window. ## Checks that held

- **Scheduling and isolation.** Source inspection confirmed an independent worker clock, equal scheduled AB/BA counts, and a fresh page and context for each run.
- **Basic statistics.** Node confirmed whole-pair resampling, per-run summaries, and the stated three-way classification and minimum-pair rule on their tested inputs.
- **History.** Source and records confirmed distinct outcomes, ordered raw stream samples, and atomic checkpoints that leave interrupted sessions identifiable.
- **Profile accounting.** In the recorded profiles, GC samples were rooted separately, idle samples were excluded from busy shares, and unavailable production mapping was labeled.

## Keep, weaken, withdraw

- **Keep** the M1 raw contrasts and intervals, the inconclusive calls at 3×120, 100,000 and 1,000,000 points, and decision 0002’s retention of the Canvas switch.
- **Weaken** “SVG by default” to a provisional choice for the tested line slice. Scope the dashboard memory result to incremental renderer PSS at DPR 2, the frame result to observed callbacks, and the uPlot timing gap to the configurations measured.
- **Withdraw** the claims that raster explains the resize CPU gap, that simplification bounds every chart by width alone, that the SVG theme change needs no runtime work, and that production normalization and GC explain the 100,000-point gap or settle the performance budgets. The recorded heap, DOM and layout results also contradict the claim that only resize CPU favored Canvas.
