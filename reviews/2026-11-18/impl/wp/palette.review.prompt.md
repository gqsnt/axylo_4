The risk here is not taste; it is that a palette "proven" accessible rests on wrong arithmetic. Check, most important first:

1. **The colour mathematics, independently.** Re-derive from your own knowledge, not from `color.mjs`: sRGB transfer function and linearisation, WCAG 2.x relative luminance and contrast ratio, OKLab/OKLCH conversion matrices, the colour-difference measure used and its scale, and the colour-vision-deficiency simulation (which model, which matrices, at what severity, applied in which space). Write your own implementation and compare numbers with the author's for all 16 colours and all pairs. Report every disagreement larger than rounding, and which side is wrong. State plainly which constants you are confident of from memory and which you are not.
2. **Do the thresholds mean what the spec says?** For each of the six constraints in the tests: is the quantity tested the one A5 names; is the threshold on the scale A5 uses; is "worst case over deficiencies" computed over the right set; are translucent fills composited in the right space over the right background. Is any constraint vacuous or weaker than stated (for example a difference measured after rounding to 8 bits, or a hue comparison that wraps wrongly at 360°)?
3. **Is the claimed measured table right?** Recompute every row of the table in the report and the decision record with your implementation.
4. **Are the tests able to fail?** For each test, name a palette change that violates the stated rule and check the test catches it. Are expected thresholds taken from the spec or tuned to the result (the author says most constraints sit within 5% of the limit)?
5. **The feasibility claims.** The author says the lightness spread (three deep colours on white; a medium-dark first blue in the dark scheme) is forced by the constraints, and that relaxing two thresholds to 0.08 and 0.12 did not help. Is the search exhaustive over what it claims (grid resolution, bounds, pruning soundness in the branch and bound)? Can you exhibit a palette that meets all six constraints with a visibly more even lightness, or show why none exists at this grid? If the simulation model or severity is stricter than A5 requires, say so: that would be the cheapest way to a better-looking palette.
6. **Thin lines and small marks.** The 2 px line figures (about 2.5:1 at pixel ratio 1 for the three lightest light-scheme colours): is the method sound, and does A5 or WCAG 1.4.11 apply to the antialiased average or to the stroke colour? State what the spec text requires, not what would be convenient.
7. **The decision record**: statements stronger than the evidence; anything about other libraries' palettes that is stated as fact but recalled from memory.

For each finding: severity (B the accessibility claim is false, M major, m minor), file and line, the numbers, and the fix. At most 14 findings. No praise.
