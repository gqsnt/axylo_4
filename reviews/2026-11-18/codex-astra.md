## Findings

### B1. The release must pass performance gates before the method for measuring them exists

- **Where:** `02-product-spec.md` P1–P7 and §7, `03-plan-typescript-release.md` M1, M6 and M8, `04-plan-performance.md` opening and §6.
- **Evidence — Observed:** M6 requires P1–P4 to pass in the Phase 1 probe. That probe never times hover, never creates 24 charts, and never measures retained memory. The spec says Phase 3 will replace the method *before* release gating. Phase 3 starts after the TypeScript release works end to end. P5 is also supposed to inform the renderer choice in M1. [Product budgets](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/02-product-spec.md:145) · [M6 outcomes](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/03-plan-typescript-release.md:286) · [Renderer fallback](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/03-plan-typescript-release.md:339) · [Phase 3 dependency](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/04-plan-performance.md:3)
- **Change:** Move benchmark instrumentation, the reference-machine record and baseline comparison adapters to M0. Run small-chart and 24-chart renderer prototypes in M1. Establish input-to-display, streaming and memory measurements before M6. Keep provisional targets visibly provisional until calibrated. Make M8 consume a criterion-to-test manifest with actual pass, fail and unsupported results. “Pass every column” cannot be a release rule for descriptive columns such as tick-label format.

### B2. The D3 scale benchmark puts most of the million-point dataset outside the plot

- **Where:** `01-landscape.md` §5.3, D9–D10; `04-plan-performance.md` §§1 and 3.4.
- **Evidence — Observed:** The D3 adapter fixes the lower y bound at zero for every line chart. Running the checked-in deterministic generator gives **37,857 negative values at 100,000 points and 745,727 at 1,000,000**. The latter dataset ranges from −454.88 to 164.64. The stream adapter also advances the x-domain without redrawing the axis and leaves the y-domain fixed. Its 0.4 ms p95 buys a chart with stale time labels. [D3 scale and axis construction](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/libs/d3.js:23) · [D3 scale and stream adapters](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/libs/d3.js:96) · [Seeded workload](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/data.js:44)
- **Change:** Withdraw the comparable-performance interpretation of the D3 rows. Use the finite extent of the visible data, update the axes, and rerun. For **every** adapter, verify the visible domain, newest and oldest timestamp, window length, gap and an injected extremum before admitting its timing. Keep a marks-only benchmark if desired, with every adapter doing marks-only work.

### B3. The benchmark times different amounts of work under the same column heading

- **Where:** `01-landscape.md` §5.3, `02-product-spec.md` P1–P4, `04-plan-performance.md` §1.
- **Evidence — Observed:** Fixture generation and module loading finish before the timer. Adapter reshaping falls inside it. Creation awaits the adapter, then waits two `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks. React forces a synchronous commit. ApexCharts and Plotly await creation, but the streaming timer ignores the promise returned by Plotly and the ApexCharts adapter discards its update completion. uPlot’s recorded push p50/p95 is **0.0/0.1 ms**; its pinned implementation queues a commit in a microtask. The producer waits for rAF before sending the next sample, so a slow consumer gets a slower feed. There is one 300-update stream run per configuration. [Timer and producer](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/main.js:20) · [React mount](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/libs/_react.jsx:4) · [ApexCharts update](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/libs/apexcharts.js:63) · [Plotly update](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/research/harness/src/libs/plotly.js:33) · [uPlot 1.6.32 commit implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leeoniya/uPlot/1.6.32/src/uPlot.js)
- **Evidence — Inferred:** The saved columns support “elapsed time through two callbacks” and “synchronous push-call duration.” They cannot establish first correct paint, total append CPU, sustained 60 Hz throughput, or a count of dropped frames. A 20 ms callback interval is one late interval and can span several display opportunities.
- **Change:** Publish input conversion, synchronous submission, committed chart state and first verified presentation separately. Drive a second stream from an independent 60 Hz clock with sequence numbers. Measure backlog, coalesced samples, latest displayed sequence and its age. Retain the rAF-paced test as a different workload. Remove “appends in under 0.1 ms” as the basis for P3.

### B4. Decimated drawing data cannot supply the promised exact exploration experience

- **Where:** `02-product-spec.md` F2, F5, I1, A1–A4; `03-plan-typescript-release.md` §§2.1–2.2.
- **Evidence — Observed:** The plan derives accessibility from scene datum tags, while a dense line retains first, minimum, maximum and last per pixel column. It promises that every spike survives, a table drawn from normalized data, and point-by-point navigation. [Acceptance criteria](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/02-product-spec.md:74) · [Pipeline](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/03-plan-typescript-release.md:154) · [Decimation claim](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/03-plan-typescript-release.md:173)
- **Evidence — Inferred:** Two local peaks and a gap can occupy one column. Four retained points cannot encode all three features and their order in general. A keyboard user can also request an ordinary sample absent from the scene. An O(visible samples) scan each frame still grows with the window, even if drawing commands are bounded by width.
- **Change:** Make normalized samples, validity and stable sample IDs authoritative for lookup, summary, table and focus. Derive drawing geometry separately. Specify gap boundaries, sample order and ties, CSS versus device pixels, and the guarantee as “preserves the per-column range of each contiguous visible run.” Test multiple extrema and gaps in one column, inverted extrema order, zoomed boundaries and a focused sample omitted from the drawing. Measure scan cost separately from draw cost.

### M5. The replacement performance metrics still cannot establish several release budgets

- **Where:** `04-plan-performance.md` §3.1; `02-product-spec.md` P1, P4, P5.
- **Evidence — Observed:** Completion is the first rAF following a stable canvas hash. Comparators use SVG, and Axylo’s labels and tooltip are DOM. The plan uses Event Timing for pointer latency, Long Animation Frames for blocking, and a forced synchronous flush for deferred operations. [Metric definitions](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/04-plan-performance.md:39)
- **Evidence — Inferred:** A blank canvas can be stable. A finished canvas can accompany missing axes or a stale tooltip. Pixel readback can itself force expensive work. Continuous `pointermove` and `wheel` are excluded from Event Timing. A sequence of 30 ms frames can miss repeated 60 Hz deadlines without producing a 50 ms long-frame entry. A forced layout does not establish presentation. [W3C Event Timing §1.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/event-timing/#sec-events-exposed) · [W3C Long Animation Frames](https://www.w3.org/TR/long-animation-frames/)
- **Change:** Give each workload a primary metric, a start event and a verifiable end state. For creation, distinguish first meaningful chart from settled chart. For hover, send timestamped input to a known datum and identify the first displayed frame containing its expected tooltip. Use browser traces or sampled frame capture to validate a lightweight timing hook in separate instrumented runs. Report short-frame pacing and long-frame blocking independently. Record unavailable metrics explicitly per engine.

### M14. The statistical protocol can reward measurement noise and hide regressions

- **Where:** `04-plan-performance.md` §§3.3, 5 and 6.
- **Evidence — Observed:** Runs always alternate ABAB. A session’s observed A/A difference becomes its noise floor. The plan specifies a bootstrap interval for medians but gates tail latency and rare bad frames too. It requires 20 repetitions, 50 for short operations, yet PRs use 10 and nightlies use 20. One fewer slow frame in 300 can qualify a change for shipping. “No transfer” sits alongside comparisons to stored prior nights and releases. [Statistics](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/04-plan-performance.md:64) · [Shipping rules](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/04-plan-performance.md:110) · [Regression gates](/home/canarit/projects/axylo/docs/04-plan-performance.md:125)
- **Evidence — Inferred:** A single A/A median difference can be near zero on a noisy machine. The hundreds of frames in one session share the same browser, workload and thermal history. Treating them as independent exaggerates confidence. Ten percent per PR also leaves room for repeated smaller regressions.
- **Change:** Randomize balanced AB/BA blocks. Bootstrap paired **runs** or blocks, preserving their frame sequences. Estimate an A/A distribution across blocks and sessions. Predeclare primary metrics, practical tolerances, tail/count intervals and when an independent rerun confirms a failure. Use PR smoke as screening, rebuild the merge base and last release on the same runner for confirmation, and track cumulative drift against a fixed release. Distinguish “inconclusive” from “passed.”

- **Evidence — Inferred:** A 5% win on a 0.1 ms operation is 5 μs. A frequent 40 ms stall may matter more than a rare million-point render.
