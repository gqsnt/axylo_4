## Next steps after the 2026-11-21 run

Written by the Claude coordinator at the owner's request ("reflect on next steps"), for the Codex coordinator to carry out. Medians of 20 runs per renderer, SVG being the default; budget targets from the spec's P rows.

1. **Make the owner's decision reproducible.** `bench/decision.mjs decide` marks every endpoint "ineligible" because of load. Add an explicit owner override (for example `--accept-load "<reason>"`) that judges these sessions by the plan's other rules (controls, noise bands, intervals at 99.4% and 99.375%, tolerances) and writes the override and its reason into the decision file. Then run `decide` for both families on `bench/decision/no-idle-2026-11-21/` and commit the verdicts.
2. **The renderer decision needs a rule the plan does not have.** The software family favours SVG on all eight timing endpoints (by 5% to 43%); by the plan's rule 1, canvas probably loses there. The GPU family favours canvas on the two resizes (424 against 475 ms; 2,082 against 2,290 ms), the dashboard at pixel ratio 2 and memory, with first-chart timings nearly tied. The plan says how to judge each family but not what to do when they disagree. A deeper analysis (gpt-6.2-astra) should apply the rules endpoint by endpoint with the intervals from step 1 and propose a resolution; the choice is the owner's. Facts that matter: the canvas renderer costs 2,269 bytes gzipped (rule 3 removes it from the bundle if nothing separates them and it costs more than 0.5 KiB, which would give back size margin); the default headless browser rasterises in software, but most readers' browsers raster on a GPU.
3. **Budgets measured by this run** (SVG): P2 at 100,000 points 43.9 ms (budget 60, met); P3 1.82 ms per update (budget 2, met) with 0 of 20 runs late (met); P5 97.6 ms (budget 200, met); **P1 29.4 ms (budget 10 ms, missed by about 3×)**; **P8 81.4 ms (budget 80 ms, missed by 1.7%)**. Under the owner's gate a missed budget blocks the release.
4. **Budgets still unmeasured:** P2 at 1,000,000 points, P4 (pointer over 100,000 points), P7 (owned bytes and the 1,000-cycle heap trend). Run the budget endpoints (`bench/README.md`, "Budget endpoints outside both families"); ask the owner whether the same "count it" applies to running them without the idle wait.
5. **P1 is the real problem.** First render of the 3×120 trend chart took 17.4 ms in the M1 session of 2026-11-18 and 29.4 ms now (both under load), so part of the gap may be features added since (interaction layer, table, readout). Profile it (production build, the plan's method), find where the 29 ms go, and either make it fast enough with tests that do not weaken anything, or bring the measurement and a written reason to the owner, who alone may change a budget. uPlot drew the same chart in about 8.6 ms in the M1 session, with no accessibility layer.
6. **P8 is marginal** (1.4 ms over 80 on a loaded machine): a small saving in compact-chart creation likely suffices; profile before changing anything.
7. **Size** stays a constraint for any performance fix (115.48 / 96.32 KiB against 116 / 98).
8. Then the release commit as below.

## Known limits

- Not testable on this machine: Safari (macOS, iOS), Edge, Windows, macOS, a real phone. No screen reader was run (owner's decision).
- GPU rendering can be tested here (headless Chrome with `--enable-gpu --use-angle=gl --ignore-gpu-blocklist`, or headed); the benchmark's gpu family does this.
- Documented product limits: `apps/docs/pages/stability.md`, "Known limits".
- Open, not scheduled: values around 1e-43 and smaller can get mismatched or missing ticks; a click does not pin in a strip readout on line charts under about 170 px of plot (decision 0004 allows it); the scatter lookup build got slower in its author's measurement (200,000 points: 44.8 to 78.1 ms), to weigh in the performance pass; view/scene finding 10 (stack rebuilt over the whole frame per revision) deferred to performance work.
