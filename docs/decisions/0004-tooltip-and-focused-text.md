# Decision 0004: where the tooltip goes, and when the focused point's text changes

Status: decided by the coordinator on 2026-11-18 after the second review of the interaction layer (`reviews/2026-11-18/impl/wp/core3.interaction.review.md`, new findings 3 and 4). Not rehearsed with a screen reader or a screen magnifier; both parts need that before they are called verified.

## 1. The tooltip is anchored to the datum and never takes the pointer (revision 3)

Revision 1 (placed across the reading axis, offset from the pointer, interactive while hovered) was implemented three times and still failed the acceptance tests: because the tooltip's position followed the pointer it moved away when approached, because it took pointer events it stalled the readout or swallowed the click whenever a move landed on it, and it could cover controls and the focus ring. Revision 2 removes the causes instead of tuning distances. Revision 3 (after the final review, `reviews/2026-11-18/impl/wp/core-final.interaction.review.md`) replaces revision 2's separate hold rules with one, and adds rules 8 to 10 and the limits in rules 5 and 6.

**Problem.** WCAG 1.4.13 requires content shown on hover to stay when the pointer moves onto it. Spec I1 requires the readout to name the datum at the pointer on every move, the tooltip not to cover the active mark, to stay inside the viewport, and to fall back to a fixed readout when it cannot fit. On a line chart the whole plot picks, so anything that takes pointer events inside the plot blocks picking.

**Rules.**

1. **Position depends on the datum only.** For the datum on show, the tooltip is placed against the bounding box of that datum's active marks (all series at that x for line, area and bar; the point for scatter; the slice for donut), enlarged by the focus ring's width. The pointer's own position never enters the computation, so the tooltip does not move while the datum is unchanged, and it cannot run away from an approaching pointer.
2. **Candidates, in order:** above the box, below it, then to the right and to the left of it (for horizontal bars: right, left, above, below). Along the other axis it is centred on the box and shifted only as far as needed. A candidate is valid when the tooltip lies wholly inside the plot rectangle, inside the viewport and inside every clipping ancestor, and overlaps neither the box nor any control. The first valid candidate is used. For a donut the first candidate is the hole, when the tooltip fits inside it.
3. **A hovered tooltip never takes pointer events.** It is `pointer-events: none`. The plot keeps receiving every move, so picking never stalls at any speed, and a click goes to the plot and selects the datum under the pointer. Moving the pointer onto the tooltip therefore cannot hide it: on line, area and bar the tooltip stays for as long as the pointer's position along the reading axis still picks the same datum, which a straight approach across the reading axis does; on scatter and donut, while the pointer is inside the tooltip's rectangle the datum is held (computed from coordinates in the plot's own move handler, not by letting the tooltip take the event).
3b. **A rest holds the datum along the way to the content (revision 3).** Rule 3 alone lets the content change whenever the straight path to it crosses positions that pick something else, and the final review found such paths for every placement: a tooltip beside a tall bar, a donut's tooltip in the hole, a scatter point's tooltip, a tooltip above a point in a dense line near the plot's edge, and the strip under a small plot. So one rule covers all of them, replacing the per-placement cases of revision 2 (including the unconditional hold inside a scatter or donut tooltip's rectangle, which is withdrawn): once the pointer has rested on a datum for 250 ms (no move larger than 2 CSS px, measured in viewport pixels), that datum is held while the pointer stays inside the corridor between its resting position and the rectangle of the content shown for it (the floating tooltip, or the strip in readout mode), that is, the convex hull of the point and the rectangle, and inside the rectangle itself. Leaving the corridor resumes picking on that same move. A pointer that has not rested is never held, in any placement, so a sweep never stalls.
   - **The hold affects what is shown, never what is activated.** A click or tap always picks afresh from the pointer's position; if that is a different datum from the one held, the click selects and pins the datum under the pointer. A click on empty space (a donut's hole, outside a scatter point's radius) selects nothing.
   - **The hold belongs to one datum, one geometry and one presentation.** It ends when the displayed frame changes that datum's anchor or the content's rectangle, when the presentation switches between floating and strip, on a pin, on Escape, when the pointer leaves the plot and the content, and on any rebuild. After it ends, picking is from the pointer's position at once, without waiting for a move.
4. **Rows are not controls while hovered.** A hovered tooltip is text. Clicking or tapping the plot pins the tooltip for that datum; a pinned tooltip takes pointer events, stays until Escape, a click elsewhere, or its datum leaves the displayed frame, and its rows are buttons of at least 24 × 24 CSS px (WCAG 2.5.8) that select a series. Keyboard focus shows the same tooltip by the same placement; it is never over the focus ring because the box includes the ring.
5. **Readout mode is decided by layout, not by hover.** When the plot, laid out without a strip, would be narrower than 240 CSS px or shorter than 140 CSS px, the figure is in readout mode: a strip of two text lines (two actual line heights of the strip's own font, whatever the page's text size) is reserved under the plot in the layout, and the content that would be in the tooltip is written there as running text ("x: series value; series value; +N more"), wrapping to the two lines and ending in "+N more" when it does not fit; at least the x and one row are always shown. The strip is part of the figure's flow: it covers nothing and nothing covers it. In readout mode there is no floating tooltip. A pinned datum makes the strip's entries buttons of 24 px height. If reserving the strip would leave the plot under the minimum of spec L8 (80 px high), no strip is reserved and pointing shows no readout at all: the datum is still highlighted, and its values are in the keyboard reading and the table. Such a plot is never put into floating mode.
6. **When no candidate is valid outside readout mode** (a tooltip taller than the room around a tall stack, for example), rows are dropped from the end and counted in "+N more" until a candidate is valid. The tooltip is never wider or taller than the visible part of the plot (its content wraps; the visible part is the plot rectangle cut by the viewport and by every clipping ancestor's real clipping edge). If no candidate is valid with one row, the candidate that overlaps the active box least is used, shifted along its free axis as far as needed to avoid every control and every focus ring: controls and focus rings are never covered, in any case. This is the only case in which the active mark may be partly covered, and the table still has every value.
7. Escape dismisses a hovered or pinned tooltip without moving the pointer, until the pointer reaches a different datum. One Escape does one thing: while a tooltip, a pin or a drag is active, the chart consumes the key and nothing else reacts to it (a surrounding dialog stays open, single-series reading stays on); the next Escape goes to whatever is next.
8. **A pin is one datum.** The pin remembers the datum the reader chose. Other data that come to share its position are listed with it while it is displayed; when it leaves the displayed frame or is hidden, the pin ends, whatever else is at that position. A dismissal is remembered for that datum in the same way. With `tooltip: false` nothing can be pinned: a click still selects and takes focus.
9. **Pinned content is real.** A pinned tooltip or strip is exposed to assistive technology with its row buttons named by their text; rows keep their element across redraws while their identity is unchanged, so a press that spans a data update still completes.
10. **Coordinates.** Picking, anchors, clipping and placement use one mapping between the plot's layout pixels and viewport pixels, taken from fractional sizes, so a translated or scaled ancestor (any positive scale, uniform or not) is exact. Rotated, skewed or mirrored ancestors are not supported in 1.0; the limits section of the spec says so.

Rejected: revision 1; an interactive hovered tooltip with a "safe triangle" (still ambiguous when the reading axis and the approach coincide); always using the readout (loses the association between the values and the mark on plots that have room).

What "can itself be hovered" (spec I1) means under this decision, for the tests: the pointer can be moved straight from the datum onto the tooltip's rectangle, and the tooltip is still shown, for the same datum, when the pointer is inside it. It does not mean the tooltip is the hit-test target.

## 2. The focused point's text changes only for a reason the reader would name

**Problem.** The plot's keyboard proxy is a native range whose value text describes the focused point, ending with its position ("Point 4 of 120"). While data streams in, the count changes on every append, so the text of a focused control changes without the reader acting. Spec A7 forbids unrequested announcements; whether a given screen reader speaks such a change cannot be tested here.

**Rule.** The range's bounds and numeric value are always kept true to the displayed frame, silently. Its value text is rewritten only when:

1. the reader moves (a key, an assistive adjustment, a pointer or touch selection that takes focus),
2. the focused datum's own values change (the same x and series now read differently),
3. the focused datum leaves the frame (the text must then say where focus went), or
4. the plot regains focus.

An append that only changes the count or the ordinal of an unchanged datum does not rewrite the text. The position in the text can therefore be out of date until the next of the four events; it is recomputed then.

To check with a screen reader when one is available (added to decision 0001's list): idle appends with the plot focused are silent; an eviction of the focused point is spoken once; an increment immediately after an append lands on the expected neighbour and reads the current count.
